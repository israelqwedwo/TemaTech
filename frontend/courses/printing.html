Course Overview

The Printing Craft Engineering Technology program provides comprehensive training in both traditional and modern printing technologies. Students learn offset printing, digital printing, screen printing, and print finishing processes. The curriculum covers pre-press operations (design and plate making), press operation, and post-press finishing, preparing graduates for careers in the printing and publishing industry.

With the printing industry evolving rapidly, the program includes training on digital printing technologies, computer-to-plate systems, and workflow automation. Students gain hands-on experience with professional printing equipment and software used in commercial printing establishments.

What You Will Learn

  • Introduction to Printing: History of printing, printing processes, materials, and industry overview.
  • Pre-press Operations: Layout design, typesetting, image processing, color separation, proofing, and plate making using computer-to-plate (CTP) technology.
  • Graphic Design Software: Adobe Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, and CorelDRAW for print production.
  • Color Theory and Management: Color models (CMYK, RGB), color matching, Pantone systems, and color calibration.
  • Offset Printing: Sheet-fed and web offset presses, press setup, ink and water balance, registration, and quality control.
  • Digital Printing: Digital press operation, variable data printing, short-run printing, and on-demand publishing.
  • Screen Printing: Screen preparation, stencil making, registration, and printing on various substrates (paper, fabric, plastic, metal).
  • Large Format Printing: Banners, posters, signage, and vehicle wraps using large format printers.
  • Finishing Operations: Cutting, folding, binding (saddle stitch, perfect binding, spiral binding), trimming, and laminating.
  • Paper and Substrates: Paper types, weights, finishes, and selection for different print jobs. Other substrates like card, vinyl, and specialty materials.
  • Inks and Coatings: Ink types (oil-based, UV, water-based), mixing, matching, and special coatings (varnish, UV coating, lamination).
  • Quality Control: Print inspection, color matching, density measurement, and quality standards in printing.
  • Maintenance: Routine maintenance of printing equipment, cleaning, and troubleshooting common problems.
  • Print Estimation: Calculating costs, paper requirements, and production time for print jobs.
  • Health and Safety: Safe handling of chemicals, ventilation, personal protective equipment, and workshop safety.

Practical Training

Students receive extensive hands-on training in our print workshop:

  • Operating offset printing presses
  • Running digital printing equipment
  • Screen printing on various materials
  • Large format printing exercises
  • Pre-press work including design and plate making
  • Finishing operations (cutting, folding, binding)
  • Color matching and ink mixing
  • Producing actual print jobs (business cards, flyers, brochures, posters)
  • Final project: Complete print production from design to finished product

Career Opportunities

Graduates of this program can work in various roles including:

  • Printing Technician: Operating and maintaining printing presses
  • Press Operator: Running offset or digital printing presses
  • Pre-press Specialist: Preparing files and making plates for printing
  • Digital Printer: Operating digital printing equipment
  • Screen Printer: Producing screen-printed items (t-shirts, signs, posters)
  • Large Format Printer: Producing banners, signs, and vehicle graphics
  • Finishing Operator: Running cutting, folding, and binding equipment
  • Print Shop Manager: Managing printing operations
  • Quality Control Inspector: Ensuring print quality standards
  • Print Estimator: Calculating costs and quoting jobs
  • Self-employed: Starting your own printing business
  • Graphic Designer (with additional skills): Creating designs for print

Industries Hiring Printing Professionals

  • Commercial Printers: Companies producing business cards, flyers, brochures, magazines
  • Publishing Houses: Book and magazine publishers
  • Packaging Companies: Producing boxes, labels, and packaging materials
  • Newspaper Printing: Daily and weekly newspaper production
  • Sign and Display Companies: Producing banners, signs, and displays
  • Textile Printing: Printing on fabrics and garments
  • In-house Print Shops: Corporate, government, and educational institution print departments
  • Advertising Agencies: Producing marketing materials
  • Stationery Companies: Producing notebooks, diaries, and stationery items

Specializations

Graduates can develop expertise in specialized areas:

  • Packaging Printing: Specializing in printing for boxes, cartons, and labels
  • Textile Printing: Fabric and garment printing
  • Large Format Printing: Banners, vehicle wraps, and signage
  • Digital Print Specialist: Variable data printing and on-demand publishing
  • Wide Format Specialist: Architectural and engineering prints
  • Print Buying: Managing print procurement for organizations
