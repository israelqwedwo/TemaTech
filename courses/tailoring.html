Course Overview

The General Tailoring program focuses on practical garment construction and alteration skills. Students learn to create custom garments for men, women, and children, and perform professional alterations. From measuring and fitting to finishing techniques, the curriculum covers all aspects of tailoring for both traditional and modern clothing.

This program is ideal for students who want to develop practical sewing skills for employment in the garment industry or to start their own tailoring business. Students learn to work with various fabrics, take accurate measurements, create patterns, and construct garments that fit perfectly. The program emphasizes hands-on practice with both hand and machine sewing techniques.

What You Will Learn

  • Introduction to Tailoring: Overview of the tailoring profession, tools of the trade, and workshop organization.
  • Sewing Machine Operation: Setting up and operating industrial and domestic sewing machines, troubleshooting common problems, and routine maintenance.
  • Hand Sewing Techniques: Various hand stitches (running stitch, backstitch, slip stitch, hemming stitch), button sewing, and hand finishing.
  • Measuring and Fitting: Taking accurate body measurements for different garment types, understanding fit issues, and making adjustments.
  • Pattern Making: Drafting basic patterns, pattern grading, and adapting patterns for different sizes and styles.
  • Fabric Knowledge: Understanding different fabric types (cotton, linen, silk, wool, synthetics), fabric properties, and selecting appropriate fabrics for garments.
  • Cutting Techniques: Layout planning, pattern placement, fabric cutting techniques for different materials.
  • Men's Wear: Construction of shirts, trousers, shorts, jackets, and traditional men's wear (smocks, kaftans, agbadas).
  • Women's Wear: Construction of dresses, skirts, blouses, trousers, and traditional women's wear (kaba, slit, etc.).
  • Children's Wear: Making clothes for children of different ages, considering growth and comfort.
  • Alterations: Hemming, taking in, letting out, shortening/lengthening sleeves and trousers, waist adjustments, and repairs.
  • Finishing Techniques: Seam finishes, edge finishes, buttonholes, zipper installation, and pressing techniques.
  • Lining and Interfacing: When and how to use lining, interfacing, and interlining in garments.
  • Special Occasion Wear: Making wedding and special occasion garments (dresses, gowns, suits).
  • Customer Service: Communicating with clients, understanding their needs, providing quotations, and managing orders.
  • Business Skills: Pricing garments, record keeping, marketing your tailoring business, and customer relations.

Practical Training

Students receive extensive hands-on practice in our tailoring workshop:

  • Sewing machine operation and maintenance exercises
  • Hand stitching practice for various techniques
  • Measuring practice on different body types
  • Pattern drafting and cutting exercises
  • Construction of sample garments (apron, simple skirt, basic shirt)
  • Complete garment construction projects
  • Alteration exercises on ready-made garments
  • Finishing and pressing techniques
  • Final project: Construct a complete outfit (e.g., shirt and trousers for men, dress for women, or traditional outfit)

Entry Requirements

  • Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE) with minimum aggregate as set by the Ghana Education Service
  • Good passes in English, Mathematics, and Integrated Science
  • Interest in sewing and working with fabrics
  • Good hand-eye coordination and attention to detail

Career Opportunities

Graduates of this program can work in various roles including:

  • Tailor: Making custom garments for individual clients
  • Seamstress: Sewing and alterations services
  • Alterations Specialist: Working in dry cleaners or clothing stores
  • Bespoke Garment Maker: Creating high-end custom clothing
  • Clothing Repair Technician: Repairing damaged garments
  • Boutique Assistant: Providing alterations in retail stores
  • Garment Factory Worker: Working in clothing manufacturing
  • Theatre Costume Assistant: Creating and maintaining costumes
  • Self-employed: Starting your own tailoring shop
  • Wedding and Bridal Specialist: Making wedding dresses and bridal party attire
  • Traditional Wear Specialist: Specializing in traditional African garments

Industries Hiring Tailors

  • Tailoring Shops: Employment in established tailoring businesses
  • Dry Cleaners and Laundries: Alteration services
  • Clothing Retailers: In-store alterations departments
  • Garment Manufacturing: Factories producing clothing
  • Theatre and Film: Costume departments
  • Hotels: Uniform maintenance and alterations
  • Bridal Shops: Wedding gown alterations
  • Self-Employment: Your own tailoring business

Garments You'll Learn to Make

  • Men's Wear: Shirts, trousers, shorts, suits, traditional smocks, kaftans, agbadas, batakari
  • Women's Wear: Dresses (various styles), skirts (pencil, A-line, gathered), blouses, tops, trousers, kaba and slit, traditional prints
  • Children's Wear: Dresses for girls, shirts and shorts for boys, school uniforms

  • Accessories: Bags, aprons, head ties, simple accessories
