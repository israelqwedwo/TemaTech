Course Overview

The Welding and Fabrication Engineering Technology program provides comprehensive training in all major welding processes and metal fabrication techniques. Students learn to work with various metals including mild steel, stainless steel, and aluminum, producing high-quality welded joints and fabricated structures for industrial, commercial, and residential applications.

From basic arc welding to advanced TIG welding, the curriculum covers all essential skills needed for a career in welding and metal fabrication. Blueprint reading, metallurgy, quality control, and safety practices are integral parts of the training. Graduates will be prepared for careers in construction, manufacturing, shipbuilding, pipeline welding, and structural fabrication.

What You Will Learn

  • Safety in Welding: Personal protective equipment (PPE), workshop safety, ventilation requirements, fire prevention, and safe handling of welding equipment and gases.
  • Arc Welding (SMAW): Shielded Metal Arc Welding techniques, electrode selection, polarity, arc striking, bead running, and welding in various positions (flat, horizontal, vertical, overhead).
  • MIG Welding (GMAW): Gas Metal Arc Welding, wire feed systems, shielding gas selection, parameter setting, and applications for thin and thick materials.
  • TIG Welding (GTAW): Gas Tungsten Arc Welding, tungsten electrode preparation, filler rod selection, and precision welding of stainless steel, aluminum, and other non-ferrous metals.
  • Oxy-fuel Cutting: Setup and operation of oxy-acetylene equipment, cutting techniques, beveling, gouging, and piercing.
  • Plasma Cutting: Manual and mechanized plasma cutting, parameter selection, and cutting various thicknesses and materials.
  • Fabrication Techniques: Layout, marking, cutting, forming, bending, rolling, and assembly of metal components.
  • Blueprint Reading: Interpreting engineering drawings, welding symbols, fabrication details, and material specifications.
  • Metallurgy: Understanding metal properties, heat treatment, weldability of different metals, and effects of welding on material properties.
  • Quality Control: Weld inspection techniques, visual inspection, destructive testing, non-destructive testing (NDT), and weld defect identification.
  • Pipe Welding: Techniques for welding pipes in various positions, pipe fitting, and code requirements.
  • Structural Welding: Welding of beams, columns, trusses, and structural connections according to building codes.

Practical Training

Students spend extensive time in our welding bay, which features multiple welding stations equipped with modern welding machines. Practical exercises include:

  • Basic weld beads and practice plates in all positions
  • Butt joints, lap joints, T-joints, and corner joints
  • Fabrication projects including gates, railings, frames, and structural components
  • Pipe welding exercises in various positions
  • Aluminum and stainless steel welding practice
  • Cutting and beveling exercises with oxy-fuel and plasma
  • Weld testing and inspection exercises
  • Final project: Complete fabricated structure (gate, frame, or equipment)

Career Opportunities

Qualified welders and fabricators are in high demand across numerous industries:

  • Structural Welder: Working on building construction, bridges, and structural steel projects
  • Pipe Welder: Welding pipelines for oil, gas, water, and industrial applications
  • Fabricator: Manufacturing metal products, equipment, and structures in workshops
  • Maintenance Welder: Repairing and maintaining equipment in factories and plants
  • Underwater Welder: Specialized welding for offshore and marine applications (with additional certification)
  • Shipyard Welder: Working on ship construction and repair
  • Automotive Welder: Custom fabrication and repair in the automotive industry
  • Welding Inspector: Quality control and inspection of welded structures
  • Welding Instructor: Teaching welding skills in technical institutions
  • Self-employed: Starting your own welding and fabrication business

Industries Hiring Welders

  • Construction: Building construction, bridges, infrastructure projects
  • Manufacturing: Metal product manufacturing, equipment fabrication
  • Oil and Gas: Pipeline construction, refinery maintenance
  • Mining: Equipment repair and maintenance, structural work
  • Shipbuilding and Marine: Ship construction, repair, and maintenance
  • Automotive: Custom fabrication, repair shops
  • Aerospace: Aircraft maintenance and repair (with additional certification)
  • Agriculture: Equipment repair, custom fabrication

Certification and Advancement

Graduates can pursue additional certifications to enhance their careers:

  • Certified Welder credentials from recognized bodies
  • Specialized certifications in pipe welding, TIG welding, or structural welding
  • Welding Inspector certification
  • Advanced Diploma in Welding Engineering
